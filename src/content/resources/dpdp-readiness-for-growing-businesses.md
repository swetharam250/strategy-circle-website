Data privacy is no longer just a technology or legal concern. For growing businesses, it is becoming a core governance requirement that touches operations, contracts, customer communication, vendor management, and board-level risk.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 creates a clear need for organizations to understand what personal data they collect, why they collect it, how long they retain it, and who has access to it.

For founders and finance teams, the first step is not to overcomplicate the process. The priority is to create visibility.

A practical DPDP readiness exercise usually begins with a few core questions:

- What personal data does the business collect?
- Where is this data stored?
- Who has access to it?
- Is consent being captured appropriately?
- Are vendors handling any personal data on behalf of the business?
- Is there a process to respond to data principal requests?

Once these areas are mapped, the business can move towards policies, notices, consent workflows, vendor checks, and internal controls.

The goal is not paperwork for its own sake. The goal is to create a clear and defensible data governance structure that supports business growth, customer trust, and compliance readiness.
