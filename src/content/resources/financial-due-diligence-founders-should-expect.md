Financial due diligence is not just a review of numbers. It is an assessment of the quality, reliability, and risk profile of a business.

During an investment, acquisition, or strategic transaction, diligence teams typically examine whether the financial story presented by the business is supported by underlying records.

Common areas of review include revenue recognition, customer concentration, margins, working capital, liabilities, tax positions, related-party transactions, and the quality of management information.

For founders, the process can feel intense because diligence requests often arrive quickly and cover several years of data.

Preparation makes a significant difference.

Before entering a transaction process, businesses should ensure that financial statements are clean, reconciliations are available, tax filings are organized, material contracts are accessible, and explanations for key movements are documented.

The objective is not to make the business look perfect. It is to make the business understandable, transparent, and defensible.

A well-prepared company can move through diligence with more confidence and fewer surprises.
